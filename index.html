Overlay Board

Upload a data file or program PDF. Overlay Board organises a full card into readable past performances, tracks scratches and money moves, and shows the handful of signals that survived testing — with what each one is actually worth.

1 Bring in your data

Brisnet data file

The .DRF file from Brisnet's "Single File" comma-delimited data product — headerless, ~1,435 fields per horse. Reads post, horse, morning-line odds, Prime Power, a surface-specific speed figure, race conditions, and an exact claiming-price class-move directly from the structured data — no guessing. If .drf files show up grayed out in your file picker, that's your phone/browser not recognizing the extension, not this tool — just rename the file to end in .csv before picking it. The parser reads the file's content, never its name, so this is completely safe and changes nothing about how it's read. If your download is a .zip, unzip it first (or send me the .zip directly and I'll pull it out for you).

CSV file

Any spreadsheet export with race, horse, and odds columns. A rating column (speed figure, power rank, your own number) is optional but unlocks value ranking.

Program PDF

Built for brisnet-style past-performance sheets. It extracts post #, horse name, morning-line odds, Prime Power, a today's-surface rating, and a coarse class-move signal where the text supports it. Runs entirely in-browser — on some phones/in-app browsers this can be slow or blocked; if it hangs, use "Paste text" instead.

Paste text

Zero dependencies, works everywhere. Open the PDF in your phone's viewer, select all / copy the text, and paste it here. Same extraction as the PDF option, no library required.

2 Check & edit entries

ML odds is the morning line — it comes in with the file. Final is the live tote board, and it starts empty on purpose: you fill it in at the track. Typing a final price never overwrites the morning line, so the tool can compare the two and show you which horses got bet down. "Surface fit" is optional — the horse's rating specific to today's surface/distance (e.g. the Trf or Dis figure from the PP, not the overall Prime Power). "Class" is optional — whether today is a step down or up from recent company. "Scr" marks a scratch — check it when the track posts changes and hit Compute again: a scratched horse is left out of every calculation, and the remaining field's odds-implied probabilities renormalize automatically.

Apply scratches from the Equibase feed

Load your entries first, then bring in the track's Changes & Scratches feed: choose the file, or open it in your browser and paste the text below. Every scratched horse gets ticked in the Scr column automatically, matched on race and program number, and the card recomputes. If the file picker greys the .rss file out, that's your phone not recognizing the extension — paste the text instead, or rename it to end in .txt. The feed also carries rider changes; those are reported, never applied, because the rating blend uses the jockey's meet win rate from the data file and there is no way to recompute it for a rider the file has never seen.

★ Where the board likes a horse

One rule, and it is the only place this tool names a horse: the field's highest career earnings per start is also the market favourite. Two measures that share no input agreeing on the same animal. Across 43 races it fired 20 times and that horse won 60%, against 30% for the same favourites without the earnings backing. Earnings per start on its own is worse than backing favourites (39.5% vs 44.2%), so the agreement is the whole signal. Found by looking rather than predicted, and 60% at short odds is thin against takeout — treat it as the best-supported thing here, not as a licence.

3 Race card — every race, in race order

This tool does not pick horses. It used to, and the picks were measured to point the wrong way — across 316 scored runners the horses the model liked most won 6.2% and the ones it hated most won 35.5%. That model is gone. What is left is the market price as a benchmark, the few signals that survived testing (each shown with its record), and fast access to the past performances so you can read the race yourself.

"Clean copy" strips this tool's own scorecard — ROI, hit rates, build history, sample-size caveats — from the screen, the printout and the downloaded file. Bets and the plain disclaimer stay. Leave it off when you're working; turn it on before you hand the card to anyone else.

This switch picks which column the math reads. Morning line uses the ML that came with the file. Final odds uses whatever you typed in the Final column, falling back to the ML for any horse you haven't updated — so at the track you can fill in just the race you're standing in front of, hit Compute, and read that one card. Final odds are also what makes the money-move column work, since drift is measured against the morning line.

5 Score the benchmark against results

This grades the market favourite in each race, not a pick — the tool doesn't make picks. It is here to keep the benchmark honest and visible: expect around 30% winners and a losing ROI, because takeout is 17-20%. Any signal worth adding later has to beat this line, not beat zero.

Paste a results chart

Paste the text of a brisnet-style results chart (the "FIRST RACE… $2 Mutuel Prices…" pages). It only needs the win/place/show payout lines — copy as much of the chart as you have and drop it in below.

How to read this: odds get converted to an implied win probability and normalized across the field to strip out the track's built-in edge (the overround). If you supplied a rating column, that becomes a second, independent probability estimate by weighting each horse's share of the field's total rating — nothing fancier than that, and it's only as good as the rating you feed it. "Edge" is just the gap between the two. A positive edge means the rating thinks the horse is more likely to win than the odds imply; it is not a prediction of the outcome. This tool doesn't know track bias, scratches after your upload, trip trouble, or today's pace shape — mark scratches in the Scr column and recompute as changes come in, and read the past performances yourself before betting.
