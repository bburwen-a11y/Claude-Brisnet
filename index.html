Overlay Board

Upload a CSV or a race-program PDF. Overlay Board reads the odds, lines up any rating you give it against what the crowd is paying, and ranks where the price beats the probability.

1 Bring in your data

Brisnet data file

The .DRF file from Brisnet's "Single File" comma-delimited data product — headerless, ~1,435 fields per horse. Reads post, horse, morning-line odds, Prime Power, a surface-specific speed figure, race conditions, and an exact claiming-price class-move directly from the structured data — no guessing. If .drf files show up grayed out in your file picker, that's your phone/browser not recognizing the extension, not this tool — just rename the file to end in .csv before picking it. The parser reads the file's content, never its name, so this is completely safe and changes nothing about how it's read. If your download is a .zip, unzip it first (or send me the .zip directly and I'll pull it out for you).

CSV file

Any spreadsheet export with race, horse, and odds columns. A rating column (speed figure, power rank, your own number) is optional but unlocks value ranking.

Program PDF

Built for brisnet-style past-performance sheets. It extracts post #, horse name, morning-line odds, Prime Power, a today's-surface rating, and a coarse class-move signal where the text supports it. Runs entirely in-browser — on some phones/in-app browsers this can be slow or blocked; if it hangs, use "Paste text" instead.

Paste text

Zero dependencies, works everywhere. Open the PDF in your phone's viewer, select all / copy the text, and paste it here. Same extraction as the PDF option, no library required.

2 Check & edit entries

"Surface fit" is optional — the horse's rating specific to today's surface/distance (e.g. the Trf or Dis figure from the PP, not the overall Prime Power). "Class" is optional — whether today is a step down or up from recent company. Leave both blank to use the overall rating as-is. "Scr" marks a scratch — check it when the track posts changes (usually by mid-morning race day) and hit Compute again: a scratched horse is left out of every calculation, and the remaining field's odds-implied probabilities renormalize automatically. Scratching beats deleting, since you keep the row if the horse re-enters or you mis-marked it.

Paste a results chart

Paste the text of a brisnet-style results chart (the "FIRST RACE… $2 Mutuel Prices…" pages). It only needs the win/place/show payout lines — copy as much of the chart as you have and drop it in below.

How to read this: odds get converted to an implied win probability and normalized across the field to strip out the track's built-in edge (the overround). If you supplied a rating column, that becomes a second, independent probability estimate by weighting each horse's share of the field's total rating — nothing fancier than that, and it's only as good as the rating you feed it. "Edge" is just the gap between the two. A positive edge means the rating thinks the horse is more likely to win than the odds imply; it is not a prediction of the outcome, and ratings-based edges are especially unreliable with small samples or thin fields. This tool doesn't know track bias, scratches after your upload, trip trouble, or today's pace shape — mark scratches in the Scr column and recompute as changes come in, and read the past performances yourself before betting.
